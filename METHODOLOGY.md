# METHODOLOGY — how `corridance` was coined and chosen

## The entry

> **corridance — the mirrored sidestep two people do in a hallway, each dodging the same way twice.**

95 characters, 97 bytes — under the bounty gate of 1–140 characters.
`corridor` + `dance`, with the middle elided, so the word sounds like
something English could have grown on its own.

## The judge this entry is built for

The bounty is judged pairwise by a model briefed as a lexicographer
and dictionary editor, weighing, most important first: (1) a freshly
invented word with a one-line definition; (2) a real gap — something
English has no word for that a reader recognises at once; (3) a
definition that lands in one reading; (4) a word that is sayable,
spellable, memorable, and sounds home-grown; (5) a fresh, surprising
pairing. Ties go to the smaller text (fewer bytes), then the earlier
receipt. The field at build time was 2,320 entries, most of them
two-word blends (visible titles included `laughlag`, `lockdoubt`,
`songslip`, `typelapse`, `ghostalgia`, `Tabnesia`).

## Step 1 — 51 candidates

Each candidate was scored 1–10 on the judge's criteria 2–5: **gap**
(real, instantly recognised, no existing word), **definition**
(lands in one reading), **word** (sayable, spellable, memorable,
home-grown), **fresh** (surprising pairing). Definitions are the
exact one-liners as drafted; lengths are characters including the word.

## Step 2 — novelty checks (a word that already exists loses)

Every finalist was checked two ways on 2026-11-25: the free
dictionary API (`api.dictionaryapi.dev`, 404 = not in the dictionary)
and a web search for the exact word.

**Rejected — the word already exists with that meaning:**

| Word | Score | One-line rejection reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| miswave | 37 | Already coined with exactly this meaning: "Mis-wave: to wave back at someone you think is waving at you, but who was actually waving to someone else" — Mark Leigh, *How to Talk Teen* (2016), plus satirical "miswave epidemic" articles. The highest-scoring candidate, disqualified on novelty. |
| roomnesia | 35 | Already a widespread social-media coinage with this exact meaning (Instagram "Word of the Day", Virgin Red "Virgin Vocab", Facebook memes); the science name for it, the "doorway effect", also exists. |
| ghostdraft | 34 | GhostDraft is an established insurance software brand (Toronto, founded 2011, ghostdraft.com) — the word is taken as a product name. |
| clockloop | 34 | Already a tech term: the infamous Xbox "clockloop" reboot bug, a JACK audio loop player (ClockLoop), and a Laravel debugging tool of the same name. |

**Weakened, not a finalist:** `househush` (33) — "house hushing"
is a Cambridge Dictionary blog new word (2024) for pale-neutral
decorating, a different meaning but a near neighbour a judge may
already know.

**Clean finalists (dictionary 404, no web hit for the word):**
`corridance`, `finelag`, `byelag`, `whatstall`, `wordshock`,
`melomemory`, `callclash`, `savehoard`, `thankwave`, `noddrift`,
`pocketpat`, `doorwaltz`, `phonecuse`, `orderhearse`, `aisledance`,

Minor notes kept, not disqualifying: `finelag` appears as an
all-caps variable name (`FINELAG`, "fine in the previous round") in

papers — technical labels, never an English word in this sense.
`byelag` appears as "Bye Lag", an Android net-optimizer app — a
product name in a different sense ("goodbye to lag").

## Step 3 — pairwise tournament (top 10 clean candidates)

Field: the seven candidates scoring above 33, plus `melomemory`
(highest gap among the 33-pointers, 9) and two wildcards picked for
gap diversity, `savehoard` (digital hoarding) and `noddrift`
(social pretence). Seven further candidates tied at 33
(`thankwave`, `phonecuse`, `orderhearse`, `aisledance`,
`sneezeghost`, `muteoops`, `sockslide`) are recorded as tied
alternates; they lost the wildcard pick on gap diversity, not on
score. Each pair was decided on the judge's criteria in order
(gap → definition → word → freshness); higher total wins, ties
broken the same way. Round-robin, 9 games each:

| Place | Word | W–L | Deciding notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **corridance** | **9–0** | Beats finelag in the final on word form (criterion 4): its elided blend sounds home-grown, while "finelag" is a plain compound whose "-lag" is borrowed tech slang; its gap is physical and cross-cultural, needing no English idiom. |
| 2 | finelag | 8–1 | Loses only the final to corridance; its definition is the most perfect of the field, and its gap (the lie behind "I'm fine") is the most emotionally deep. |
| 3 | wordshock | 7–2 | Beats byelag on definition (10 vs 9); loses to the top two on gap weight. |
| 4 | byelag | 6–3 | A small moment (a two-second pause) against heavier gaps; still the punchiest word in the field at 6 letters. |
| 5 | pocketpat | 5–4 | Beats whatstall on word form (8 vs 7 — "whatstall" reads as a verb phrase, not a noun); beats doorwaltz on gap (9 vs 8). |

## Why `corridance` wins

- **Gap:** everyone has done the hallway dance — it is physical,
  wordless, and instantly recognised across ages and cultures, and
  no single word names it (only the phrase "hallway dance").
- **Definition:** one clause, one image ("mirrored sidestep …
  dodging the same way twice"), lands in a single reading.
- **Word:** ten letters, three easy syllables (COR-ri-dance),
  spelled as it sounds, no silent traps; the elision makes it feel
  grown rather than assembled, unlike the field's stapled blends.
- **Freshness:** comic and visual — a dance nobody chose — where
  most of the field is earnest or tech-bound.
- **Size:** 95 characters / 97 bytes, shorter than every other
  finalist's entry except finelag's, which helps the byte tie-break.

## The program

`main.py` writes the fixed entry literal to `/output/entry.txt`
(overridable with `MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR` for local testing), chmod
0644 so the grader can collect it as another user. No network, no
randomness, no clock: two runs are byte-identical
(`test_determinism.sh` checks this, plus that the output matches
`entry.txt` and passes the 1–140 character gate). `entry.txt`
holds the same 97 bytes with no trailing newline, so the judged
text is exactly the word and its definition.
